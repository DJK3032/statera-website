01

Founder's Operating Spine

I take technical founders from product to company.

For seed to Series A companies with a technical founder, real traction and money in the bank, but no operator. The tell: the team ships product but misses deadlines, can't hire cleanly, has no financial rhythm, and the founder is drowning in work that isn't the product. Especially strong for defense and government-adjacent startups.

A week-one diagnostic decides which three or four of the six systems matter most for your company.

Finance and Cash

Outsourced accounting stood up fast on DCAA-compliant systems from day one, plus the model, budget, burn and runway tracking, and the monthly close.

People

A real PEO chosen to scale to 30 to 40 people without a painful migration, plus the org plan, role scorecards, repeatable recruiting and onboarding, and comp bands.

Rhythm and Planning

The operating cadence, goals, and the weekly and monthly meetings that actually run the company.

Legal and Compliance

Corporate housekeeping, contracts, and the facility clearance process started early when a DD 254 is active or likely.

Tools and Data

Systems of record, so the company isn't run out of one person's head and a spreadsheet.

Go-to-Market or Delivery

The repeatable back end to sell and deliver, depending on the company.

Month one

Diagnose

Find what is broken and decide which systems matter most.

Months two to four

Build

Stand up the two or three systems that carry the company.

Final phase

Hand off

Hire and train the junior operator and transfer everything. The handoff is the deliverable.

Terms: fixed fee, scoped per engagement, paid monthly across a three to six month term. The comparison is a full-time COO you can't yet afford or find. This costs a fraction of that and leaves a trained operator in the seat.

02

Federal Market Entry

Federal revenue for commercial companies.

Plenty of established commercial companies already do work the government buys, but have never won it directly. I run the capture work for them: finding the right opportunities, saying no to the wrong ones, and writing responses that match what the government asked for.

Opportunity Triage

Screen SAM.gov and agency forecasts for work you can actually win, on fit, set-aside and size, and pass on the rest early.

RFI and Sources Sought

Responses that shape the requirement and put your company on the contracting officer's list before the RFP drops.

Proposals

Compliant, evaluator-ready proposals built from your real past performance and verified facts.

Teaming

Find the primes and subcontractors that fill your gaps, and set up the relationship before the solicitation does.
